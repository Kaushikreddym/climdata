    Dataset Catalogue¶

    Every dataset climdata can fetch, on one page: what it covers, how fine it is, where it comes from, what you need to access it, and how well it fits a crop-model workflow such as DSSAT, APSIM, WOFOST, SIMPLACE or LINTUL.

    All of them are reached the same way: set dataset= and nothing else changes. See Datasets for the call pattern and region selection.

    Which one do I need?¶

    • Calibrating at a field trial

      Use station records where you have them, and a fine grid where you don't.

      dwd · hyras · power

    • Gridded historical runs, any region

      Bias-corrected global daily weather with the full crop-model variable set.

      mswx · w5e5 · era5

    • Climate-change projections

      Bias-adjusted CMIP6 you can feed straight into a crop model.

      nexgddp · cmip_w5e5

    • Benchmarking against GGCMI

      Simulated yields from the ISIMIP crop-model ensemble, for comparison with your own runs.

      agri_isimip

    Crop-model variables by dataset¶

    A daily crop model typically needs minimum and maximum temperature, rainfall, solar radiation, and (for Penman–Monteith ET or VPD-driven transpiration) humidity and wind. The table shows which datasets supply each one.

    Tmin / Tmax Rain Radiation Humidity Wind Also
    mswx
    era5 rlds
    w5e5 rlds, ps, huss
    power
    hyras et0, evpot, soil moisture, soil temp.
    hostrada hourly only
    dwd station-dependent
    cmip
    nexgddp rlds, huss
    cmip_w5e5 rlds, ps, psl

    = available from the provider but not yet mapped in climdata.

    Units before you write a weather file

    climdata returns rsds in W m⁻² as a daily mean. Most crop models want daily radiation totals in MJ m⁻² d⁻¹, so multiply by 0.0864. Temperatures come back in °C and precipitation in mm d⁻¹.

    CO₂ is not in any of these

    None of the climate datasets carry atmospheric CO₂. For SSP runs, take the annual concentrations for your scenario from the CMIP6 forcing files (the ISIMIP3b protocol publishes them) and set them in the crop model yourself. Otherwise the CO₂-fertilisation effect is silently left out.

    Observations and reanalysis¶

    MSWX¶

    Multi-Source Weather · GloH2O · Observation
    Spatial resolution 0.1° (~11 km), global land
    Time step daily (climdata); the native product is 3-hourly
    Period 1979 – present, with near-real-time updates
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds hurs sfcWind
    Source Google Drive, one NetCDF per day per variable · gloh2o.org/mswx
    Licence CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial use only

    For crop modellers

    One of the best global choices for gridded historical simulations. It has every variable a DSSAT/APSIM weather file needs, bias-corrected against station observations at 0.1°, which is fine enough to tell apart neighbouring agro-ecological zones. The near-real-time tail also makes it usable for in-season yield forecasting. Precipitation merges gauge, satellite and reanalysis data (it is MSWEP's sibling), so rain-fed yield variance is captured better than with ERA5 alone.

    Credentials

    1. Request access to the MSWX data from GloH2O (see the link above).
    2. Create a Google Cloud service account, download its JSON key, and share the MSWX Drive folder with the service-account e-mail.
    3. Pass the key with dsinfo.MSWX.params.google_service_account=/path/to/key.json.

    You only need the key for files that aren't already on disk. The MSWX guide covers every step.

    Reference: Beck, H. E. et al. (2022). MSWX: Global 3-hourly 0.1° bias-corrected meteorological data including near-real-time updates and forecast ensembles. Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society, 103(3), E710–E732. doi:10.1175/BAMS-D-21-0145.1

    ERA5¶

    ECMWF Reanalysis v5 · Copernicus C3S · Reanalysis
    Spatial resolution 0.25° (~31 km), global
    Time step hourly, or daily statistics
    Period 1940 – present (about 5 days behind real time)
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr hurs sfcWind rsds rlds, plus pressure levels
    Source Copernicus Climate Data Store via cdsapi, mirrored to local Zarr

    For crop modellers

    Use the hourly fields when the process you model is sub-daily, for example heat stress during anthesis or hourly canopy temperature and VPD. ERA5 is not bias-corrected: precipitation shows the usual reanalysis drizzle bias (too many light-rain days, too few heavy ones), which skews infiltration and runoff in soil-water modules. For daily rain-fed simulations, prefer MSWX or W5E5, which are built on ERA5 but corrected.

    Credentials

    Needs a free Copernicus CDS account. Accept the ERA5 licence on the dataset page, then put your API key in ~/.cdsapirc. ERA5 is driven via climdata.ERA5 rather than ClimData(...).extract(); see Datasets › ERA5.

    Reference: Hersbach, H. et al. (2020). The ERA5 global reanalysis. Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society, 146(730), 1999–2049. doi:10.1002/qj.3803

    W5E5¶

    WFDE5 over land merged with ERA5 over ocean · ISIMIP · Observation
    Spatial resolution 0.5° (~55 km), global
    Time step daily
    Period 1979 – 2019
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds rlds hurs huss sfcWind ps
    Source ISIMIP repository via isimip-client · data.isimip.org

    For crop modellers

    This is the observational climate of GGCMI phase 3 and the reference that cmip_w5e5 was bias-adjusted to. If your study follows the ISIMIP3b protocol, calibrate on W5E5 and then run the cmip_w5e5 projections: the historical and future climate are then consistent by construction, and your results compare directly with the GGCMI ensemble in agri_isimip. The 0.5° grid is too coarse for field-scale calibration, and the record stops in 2019.

    Credentials

    None. Public data; install isimip-client.

    Reference: Lange, S. et al. (2021). WFDE5 over land merged with ERA5 over the ocean (W5E5 v2.0). ISIMIP Repository. doi:10.48364/ISIMIP.342217 · Cucchi, M. et al. (2020). WFDE5: bias-adjusted ERA5 reanalysis data for impact studies. Earth System Science Data, 12, 2097–2120. doi:10.5194/essd-12-2097-2020

    NASA POWER¶

    Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources · Agroclimatology · Reanalysis-derived
    Spatial resolution point query; meteorology from MERRA-2 (0.5° × 0.625°), solar from CERES/GEWEX (1°)
    Time step daily
    Period 1981 – present
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds sfcWind
    Source NASA POWER REST API, AG community · power.larc.nasa.gov

    For crop modellers

    Built for agroclimatology: the AG community endpoint that climdata queries is the one DSSAT's weather tools use. It's the fastest way to get a complete weather file for a single site anywhere, and its satellite-derived radiation is often better than nothing at data-poor sites. Being point-only, it fits site calibration rather than regional runs. Precipitation is coarse reanalysis, so check it against a local gauge before trusting simulated water stress.

    Credentials

    None.

    Reference: NASA Langley Research Center, POWER Project. power.larc.nasa.gov · Sparks, A. H. (2018). nasapower: a NASA POWER global meteorology, surface solar energy and climatology data client for R. Journal of Open Source Software, 3(30), 1035. doi:10.21105/joss.01035

    HYRAS¶

    Hydrologische Rasterdaten · Deutscher Wetterdienst · Gridded observation
    Spatial resolution 1 km, Germany (and the transboundary river basins for some variables)
    Time step daily
    Period 1951 – present (start year varies by variable)
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds hurs, plus DWD grids for et0 (FAO), evpot, soilMoist, soilTemp (5 cm)
    Source DWD open-data server · opendata.dwd.de › grids_germany/daily

    For crop modellers

    The standard choice for crop modelling in Germany. At 1 km a grid cell is close to the scale of a field trial, so it can stand in for a missing station. The extra DWD grids are unusually useful: FAO-56 reference ET (et0) can go straight into models that accept ET₀, and 5 cm soil temperature helps simulate sowing-to-emergence. HYRAS has no wind, so for Penman–Monteith inside the model either use et0 directly or add wind from dwd stations or hostrada.

    Credentials

    None. Anonymous HTTPS. Files are national annual NetCDFs, so the first download of a year is large even for a single point.

    Reference: Razafimaharo, C., Krähenmann, S., Höpp, S., Rauthe, M. & Deutschländer, T. (2020). New high-resolution gridded dataset of daily mean, minimum, and maximum temperature and relative humidity for Central Europe (HYRAS). Theoretical and Applied Climatology, 142, 1531–1553. doi:10.1007/s00704-020-03388-w · Rauthe, M. et al. (2013). A Central European precipitation climatology – Part I. Meteorologische Zeitschrift, 22(3), 235–256. doi:10.1127/0941-2948/2013/0436

    HOSTRADA¶

    Hourly Station-based and Raster-based Terrestrial Atmospheric Dataset · DWD · Gridded observation
    Spatial resolution 1 km, Germany (regular lat/lon, EPSG:4326)
    Time step hourly
    Period 1995 – present
    Variables rsds, wspd (wind speed)
    Source DWD open-data server, monthly NetCDF · opendata.dwd.de › grids_germany/hourly/hostrada

    For crop modellers

    A complement to HYRAS, not a replacement: climdata currently maps only radiation and wind. Use it to fill HYRAS's missing wind, or to drive hourly canopy photosynthesis and light-interception models that need the diurnal radiation curve rather than a daily total. Shapefile requests are clipped to the polygon's bounding box, not the polygon itself.

    Credentials

    None.

    Reference: Deutscher Wetterdienst, Climate Data Center. HOSTRADA v1.0: hourly gridded surface climate data for Germany. opendata.dwd.de

    DWD stations¶

    Deutscher Wetterdienst station network · Station observation
    Spatial resolution point; climdata picks the nearest station(s)
    Time step daily, hourly
    Period varies by station; many start before 1950
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds hurs sfcWind
    Source DWD Climate Data Center via the wetterdienst library · opendata.dwd.de/climate_environment/CDC

    For crop modellers

    The ground truth for calibrating and validating at German experimental sites. Not every station measures every variable, radiation in particular, and gaps are common, so run the series through climdata.impute before writing a weather file. A gap of a single day in Tmax stops most crop models. The DWD export format page describes the station-file layout.

    Credentials

    None. Install wetterdienst.

    Reference: Deutscher Wetterdienst, Climate Data Center. opendata.dwd.de/climate_environment/CDC · Access library: wetterdienst

    Climate projections¶

    Don't feed raw GCM output to a crop model

    A raw GCM with a 2 °C warm bias in Tmax moves flowering by weeks and wipes out the yield signal you are trying to measure. Use one of the bias-corrected products below, or correct cmip yourself with BCSD. Run several GCMs (ideally five or more) and report the spread: inter-model spread usually dominates crop-model uncertainty by mid-century.

    CMIP6 raw¶

    CMIP6 via the Pangeo cloud catalogue · Projection, raw
    Spatial resolution each model's native grid, typically ~1° (~100 km)
    Time step daily (table_id=day)
    Period historical 1850 – 2014; SSPs 2015 – 2100
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds sfcWind
    Source Google Cloud Storage via intake-esm (Pangeo CMIP6 catalogue)

    For crop modellers

    Use it when you need a model, member or scenario the bias-corrected products don't carry, or when you want to control the bias correction yourself, e.g. correcting to MSWX or HYRAS instead of a global reference. Plan on running it through climdata.sdba before it goes near a crop model.

    Credentials

    None. The data is public; install intake-esm.

    Reference: Eyring, V. et al. (2016). Overview of the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6) experimental design and organization. Geoscientific Model Development, 9, 1937–1958. doi:10.5194/gmd-9-1937-2016

    NEX-GDDP-CMIP6¶

    NASA Earth Exchange Global Daily Downscaled Projections · Projection, bias-corrected
    Spatial resolution 0.25° (~25 km), global
    Time step daily
    Period historical 1950 – 2014; ssp126 ssp245 ssp370 ssp585 2015 – 2100
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds rlds hurs huss sfcWind
    Source NASA NCCS THREDDS server (HTTP subsetting) · nccs.nasa.gov › NEX-GDDP-CMIP6

    For crop modellers

    The finest-resolution ready-to-use projections here, with about 35 GCMs and the full crop-model variable set. It's a good default for regional yield-impact studies. BCSD corrects each model's distribution against GMFD observations, but day-to-day sequencing comes from the GCM, so dry-spell lengths and heat-wave persistence are only as realistic as the parent model. Check them if drought timing drives your results. Not every model provides every variable.

    Credentials

    None.

    Reference: Thrasher, B. et al. (2022). NASA Global Daily Downscaled Projections, CMIP6. Scientific Data, 9, 262. doi:10.1038/s41597-022-01393-4

    CMIP6 ISIMIP3b (cmip_w5e5)¶

    CMIP6 bias-adjusted and downscaled to W5E5 · ISIMIP3BASD · Projection, bias-corrected
    Spatial resolution 0.5°, global (the W5E5 grid)
    Time step daily
    Period historical 1850 – 2014; ssp126 ssp370 ssp585 2015 – 2100
    Variables tas tasmin tasmax pr rsds rlds hurs sfcWind ps psl
    Models primary set: gfdl-esm4 ipsl-cm6a-lr mpi-esm1-2-hr mri-esm2-0 ukesm1-0-ll (lowercase)
    Source ISIMIP repository via isimip-client · data.isimip.org

    For crop modellers

    The climate forcing behind GGCMI phase 3. Pick it when your results should sit alongside the published global crop-model ensemble, or when you need the trend-preserving ISIMIP3BASD method, which keeps each model's warming signal per quantile. That matters for heat-stress thresholds in Tmax. Five models are few for a full uncertainty range, and the 0.5° grid smooths out topography in mountainous cropping regions.

    Credentials

    None.

    Reference: Lange, S. (2019). Trend-preserving bias adjustment and statistical downscaling with ISIMIP3BASD (v1.0). Geoscientific Model Development, 12, 3055–3070. doi:10.5194/gmd-12-3055-2019

    Crop-model output¶

    ISIMIP3b crop-model output¶

    GGCMI phase 3 via ISIMIP · `agri_isimip` · Simulated
    Spatial resolution 0.5°, global
    Time step annual, one value per growing season
    Period historical and SSP experiments, up to 2100; varies by model and forcing
    Variables yield (t ha⁻¹) biom lai evap aet plantyear
    Dimensions impact model × climate forcing × period × crop × irrigation (firr / noirr)
    Source ISIMIP repository, ISIMIP3b/OutputData/agriculture · data.isimip.org

    For crop modellers

    These aren't inputs: they're other models' answers. Use them to benchmark your own model against the GGCMI ensemble (e.g. LPJmL, pDSSAT, EPIC-IIASA, SIMPLACE-LINTUL5) driven by the same cmip_w5e5 climate, or to put your site results in a global context. Compare fully-irrigated and rain-fed runs to separate heat effects from water stress. Which model/crop/forcing combinations exist can't be guessed, so call AGRI_ISIMIP(cfg).explore() first.

    Credentials

    None.

    Reference: Jägermeyr, J. et al. (2021). Climate impacts on global agriculture emerge earlier in new generation of climate and crop models. Nature Food, 2, 873–885. doi:10.1038/s43016-021-00400-y

    The resolutions, periods and variable lists on this page come from climdata/conf/mappings/parameters.yaml. cd.explore("<NAME>") prints the same metadata at runtime. Provider periods and model availability change over time, so check the source before a large download.
